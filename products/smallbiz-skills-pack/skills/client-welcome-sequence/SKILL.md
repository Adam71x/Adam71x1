# Client Welcome Sequence

A new client's first week decides whether they trust you. This skill writes the emails that make you look organized and set boundaries politely, before any problems start.

## Step 1: Collect details (ask only for what is missing)

1. Your business name, your first name, and what you do in one sentence.
2. Client first name, company (optional), and the project or service they bought.
3. Start date, kickoff call date (if any), and key deadlines.
4. What you need from the client (for example: logins shared via a password manager, brand files, a questionnaire, a signed contract, a deposit).
5. Your working rules: office hours, reply time (for example "within 1 business day"), preferred channel, number of revisions, payment schedule.
6. Tone: warm and friendly (default), or crisp and corporate.

Never ask for or include passwords or card numbers in emails. Tell the client to share access through a secure method the owner chooses.

## Step 2: Write the sequence

| # | When | Email | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 (right after signing) | Welcome + next steps | Excitement, what happens next, what you need |
| 2 | Day 0 or 1 | Intake request | Questionnaire or asset list with a due date |
| 3 | 1 day before kickoff | Kickoff confirmation | Agenda, link placeholder, what to prepare |
| 4 | Day 5-7 | First-week check-in | Progress, one question, reassurance |
| 5 | Only if items are overdue | Friendly nudge | Remind of missing items and what it delays |

Rules for every email:
- Subject line under 50 characters, plus a preview line.
- 80 to 150 words. One clear action per email, in bold.
- Restate dates. Use `[LINK]` placeholders; never invent URLs.
- Boundaries are stated as helpful info, not warnings ("So you always know when to expect a reply...").

## Step 3: Welcome guide

Also produce a one-page "Working with [Business]" guide:

```
# Working with [Business]
Welcome, [Client]! Here's everything you need in one place.

## Your project at a glance
- Service: ...
- Start: ...   Key dates: ...

## How we'll communicate
- Best channel: ...   Reply time: ...   Office hours: ...

## What I need from you
- [ ] ... by [date]

## How revisions work
- ...

## Payments
- ...

## What happens next
1. ...
```

## Step 4: Output

Give the 5 emails in order (subject, preview line, body), then the welcome guide. Finish with a short **schedule** the owner can copy into their calendar or email tool, and a reminder that the emails are drafts to review before sending.

## Worked example

**Input:** "I'm Leah, a freelance bookkeeper (Ledgerly). New client: Marco at Bella Pizza, monthly bookkeeping starting 1 Oct. Kickoff call 2 Oct. I need read-only bank access, last 3 months of statements, and the signed engagement letter. I reply within 1 business day, Mon-Thu. Invoice on the 1st, due in 7 days."

**Output (email 1 of 5):**

> **Subject:** Welcome to Ledgerly, Marco!
> **Preview:** Here's what happens before our 2 Oct kickoff.
>
> Hi Marco,
>
> I'm so glad Bella Pizza is on board. From 1 October I'll handle your monthly bookkeeping, so you can spend less time on receipts and more time on the pizza.
>
> Here's what happens next:
> 1. **Please sign the engagement letter by 30 September:** [LINK]
> 2. I'll send a short checklist today of what I need (bank statements and read-only access).
> 3. We'll meet on 2 October for a 30-minute kickoff.
>
> So you always know when to expect me: I work Monday to Thursday and reply within one business day.
>
> Talk soon,
> Leah, Ledgerly

**Output (email 5, nudge):**

> **Subject:** Quick reminder: 2 items for your books
> **Preview:** So we can close September on time.
>
> Hi Marco, a quick nudge: I'm still missing **the July-September bank statements** and **read-only bank access**. Once I have them I can finish your first month's reconciliation. Could you send them by Friday? Reply here if anything is unclear. Thanks! Leah
